# Heart Attack Data Analysis – Exploring the Impact of Lifestyle Habits

## 📊 Project Overview
This project analyzes heart attack data from various states in India, focusing on the role of lifestyle habits such as smoking and drinking, and their correlation with heart attack rates. By utilizing SQL for data extraction, Excel for data cleaning, and Power BI for data analysis and visualization, this project aims to uncover key insights into how these habits influence heart health across the country.

## 🛠️ Tools and Technologies
- **SQL** – For extracting data from the source database.
- **Excel** – For data cleaning, profiling, and preprocessing.
- **Power BI** – For visualizing the data and performing analysis to uncover trends and insights.

## 🔍 Analysis & Key Insights
1. **Independent Analysis**: Through isolated analysis, it was found that **drinking** was the most prevalent factor contributing to heart attacks, followed by **smoking** and **obesity** (as visualized in pie charts).
2. **Outlier Observation**: **Kerala**, despite having one of the highest percentages of smokers and drinkers, reported one of the lowest heart attack rates. This suggests that factors other than smoking and drinking, such as **education**, **healthcare**, and **air quality**, may play a role in influencing heart attack rates.
3. **Drinking Consistency**: **Drinking** was identified as a more consistent factor in driving heart attacks compared to smoking. States with higher drinking rates generally had higher heart attack rates.

## 🔧 Key Components
- **Data Collection**: SQL queries were used to extract the relevant heart attack, smoking, and drinking data from the database.
- **Data Preprocessing**: Excel was used for initial data cleaning and structuring to prepare for deeper analysis.
- **Data Analysis & Visualization**: Power BI was used to explore relationships and create visualizations like pie charts, scatter plots, and bar charts.

## 🚀 Goals & Next Steps
This project serves as a starting point for understanding the relationship between lifestyle habits and heart attacks. Moving forward, I aim to expand on this analysis by exploring **predictive modeling** to predict heart attack occurrences based on lifestyle and demographic data.
